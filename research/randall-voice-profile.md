## How Randall Speaks (locked patterns)

### Sentence and punctuation
- Commas instead of em-dashes. Do not use " — " in manuscript.
- Mix of short punchy sentences and medium explanatory ones.
- Starts sections with plain hooks: "Here's the thing...", "You might be surprised...", "Here's the answer."
- Direct address: "you", "your trees", "your yard" — talks to the reader, not at them.

### Vocabulary level
- 7th–8th grade reading level. Plain words over jargon.
- Uses correct terms when needed (syconium, parthenocarpic, ostiole) then explains in common language.
- Avoids superlative adverbs: dramatically, remarkably, entirely, essentially (for emphasis), aggressively (as filler).
- Avoids dramatic or poetic phrasing. If it sounds like a nature documentary, rewrite it.

### Tone
- Warm, practical, not preachy.
- Light humor where it fits naturally (Thanksgiving wasp conversation, beetles "taking you up on it").
- Not stiff, not academic, not generic AI gardening voice.
- Confidence without lecturing — shares what works from experience.

### Randall-specific language (use when accurate)
- **fig honey** — nectar inside ripe figs
- **FigCrack** — community term for skin split showing white flesh at peak ripeness
- **collectors** / **the fig community** — not "enthusiasts" every time
- **lower Alabama**, **Gulf South**, **Zone 8** — real geography
- **Flomaton Famous**, **my orchard**, **my collection** — first person when it's his experience
- Forward references: "more on that later", "we'll get into that in the propagation chapter"

### What Randall does NOT do
- Open with "In this chapter we will..."
- Stack three adjectives on every noun
- Invent personal stories or variety experiences not confirmed
- Over-explain jokes or soften humor with disclaimers
- Use em-dashes, "delve," "robust," "vibrant," "bountiful," "thriving"
