## Key Biblical References to Figs

### Garden of Eden
- The fig leaf is the first clothing mentioned in the Bible (Genesis 3:7)
- Adam and Eve sewed fig leaves together after eating the forbidden fruit
- The fig tree is the third tree specifically named in Scripture (after the Tree of Life
  and the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil) [VERIFY exact sequence]
- This is a thematic anchor for the book's biblical thread

### The Fig Tree as Symbol
- Figs are frequently used as a symbol of peace, prosperity, and national blessing
  in the Old Testament
- "Each man under his own vine and fig tree" — appears in 1 Kings 4:25, Micah 4:4,
  Zechariah 3:10 — symbol of security and God's blessing
- The withered fig tree — Matthew 21:18-22, Mark 11:12-14, 20-25 — used by Jesus as
  a lesson on faith

### Figs in Prophecy
- The fig tree is used as a metaphor in end-times discussions (Matthew 24:32-33)
  — "Now learn the parable from the fig tree..."
- Joel 1:7 — "He has laid waste my vine and ruined my fig tree" — judgment imagery

### Figs as Food & Medicine in Scripture
- 1 Samuel 25:18 — Abigail brings a hundred clusters of raisins and two hundred cakes of figs
- 1 Samuel 30:12 — A fig cake revives an Egyptian servant who had not eaten for three days
- Isaiah 38:21 — A fig poultice prescribed for Hezekiah's illness [VERIFY]
- Jeremiah 24 — The parable of the two baskets of figs (good figs vs. bad figs)

## Historical References (Non-Biblical)

### Ancient World
- Figs are one of the earliest cultivated fruit crops — evidence dates back roughly 11,000 years
  near the Jordan Valley [VERIFY archaeological source]
- Ancient Egyptians cultivated figs extensively — depicted in tomb paintings
- Figs were a dietary staple in ancient Greece and Rome
- Greek athletes trained on figs during the original Olympic Games [VERIFY]
- Romans considered figs sacred — associated with Romulus and Remus and the Ficus Ruminalis
  (the sacred fig tree under which they were said to have been nursed) [VERIFY]

### Trade & Spread
- Figs spread from the Middle East through the Mediterranean with traders and armies
- Spanish missionaries brought figs to California in the 1700s — origin of the "Mission" fig variety
- "Brown Turkey" and "Celeste" became popular in the American South for their cold hardiness

### Modern Context
- Ficus carica is the common edible fig — originated in Western Asia
