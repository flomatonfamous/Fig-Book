## Key Concepts to Carry Into Chapter 7

### The Core Philosophy (from the guide)
- Figs are moderate feeders — "a little, often is far safer than a lot, rarely"
- Never fertilize a stressed, dormant, or drought-dry tree
- Core promise: "Feed enough for productive growth and sweet fruit without driving excess
  leaves, fruit drop, or winter damage"
- The 3-inch internode rule: if new growth nodes are more than 3 inches apart, the tree
  is already over-fed on nitrogen — stop immediately

### The Feed / Hold / Stop Framework
This maps well to Chapter 7's structure:

**FEED (Green Light):**
- Tree has 3–4 fully open leaves
- Soil is moist (not dry, not waterlogged)
- Air temp consistently above 50°F
- No active disease or pest stress
- Inside the feeding window (budbreak to late August)
- Not fertilized within the past 2 weeks (liquid) or 4–6 weeks (granular)

**HOLD (Pause and assess):**
- Transplant shock — wait 4–6 weeks after repotting
- Heat stress above 100°F
- Overwatered/waterlogged soil
- Late frost forecast after budbreak
- Visible root damage
- Fruit visibly ripening — nitrogen during ripening delays color and reduces sugar
- Just received heavy pruning — wait 2–3 weeks

**STOP (Non-negotiable):**
- Tree is fully dormant
- Soil temp below 50°F
- Salt burn showing (brown curling margins)
- pH out of range (below 5.5 or above 7.5)
- Within 6 weeks of a hard freeze
- First 30 days after bare-root planting or major root work

### Seasonal Calendar (Zones 7–10)
| Month | Stage | Feeding Strategy |
|-------|-------|-----------------|
| Jan–Feb | Dormant | STOP — no feeding |
| March | Budbreak | Light feed when 3–4 leaves open — balanced or high-N |
| Apr–May | Active growth | Full schedule every 2–4 weeks — balanced to high-N |
| June | Breba ripening + main crop set | Ease nitrogen, increase K |
| Jul–Aug | Main crop swelling | Light potassium-focused only |
| September | Late fruit finishing | STOP or hold N entirely |
| Oct–Nov | Hardening off | STOP all feeding |
| December | Dormant | STOP |

**Zone 8 specific note (Alabama, Mississippi, Georgia):**
- Fig trees in Zone 8 often push 2–3 growth flushes per season
- Feed actively in Flush 1 (March–May), moderately in Flush 2 (June)
- Do NOT feed Flush 3 (late summer) — it delays dormancy and risks winter dieback
- Trees in 7-gal+ containers can produce 40–80+ figs per season with right program

### NPK Understanding for Figs
- N (Nitrogen): drives leaf and shoot growth — deficiency shows as yellow older leaves;
  excess shows as vigorous vines with few or no fruit and tip burn
- P (Phosphorus): root development, flower/fruit set — deficiency shows as purple tint
  on leaf undersides
- K (Potassium): fruit size and quality, drought resistance — deficiency shows as leaf
  edge scorch; shift to higher K as fruit sets in June
- N focus: March–June | P focus: early spring | K focus: June–August

### Reading the Leaves (Diagnostic Table from guide)
| Leaf Appearance | Meaning |
|----------------|---------|
| Deep, glossy dark green | Nitrogen adequate — maintain program |
| Pale green / light yellow overall | Nitrogen deficiency — begin or increase feeding |
| Yellow between veins, new growth | Iron or magnesium deficiency — check pH first |
| Lush, dark green with zero fruit | Nitrogen excess — stop all N for 3–4 weeks |
| Brown margins curling inward | Salt burn or drought — flush and check moisture |
| Purple/red tint on undersides | Phosphorus deficiency or cold soil |
| Small, cupped new leaves | Zinc deficiency — micronutrient supplement |

### Fingernail Test for Soil Moisture
Push finger 2 inches into soil before every fertilizer application:
- Cool and damp, clings to finger → safe to fertilize
- Dry and crumbly → water first, wait 12 hours, then fertilize
- Soggy → do NOT fertilize; fix drainage first

### Container vs. In-Ground Key Differences
- Container: nutrients deplete faster; flush every 4–6 weeks with plain water;
  avoid granular 10-10-10 in containers (releases too fast, spikes salts)
- In-ground: established trees in good soil may need very little supplemental fertilizer;
  let leaf color guide; compost mulch ring is often sufficient
- 4x9 tree pot program: 1 tbsp Osmocote Plus in April + 1/2 tbsp refresh in June

### Organic Fertilizer Products Referenced
- Espoma Citrus-Tone (6-3-3) — all-season, very low risk
- Dr. Earth Fruit Tree (5-5-2) — soil building, new plants
- Neptune's Harvest Fish & Seaweed (2-3-1) — early season drench/foliar
- Worm Castings (~1-0-0) — top-dress, soil health
- Blood Meal (12-0-0) — quick spring N boost, medium risk, once per season max
- Bone Meal (3-15-0) — root and fruit development
- Aged Compost/Manure — soil structure and mulch

### Synthetic Fertilizer Products Referenced
- Osmocote Plus (15-9-12) — container season-long slow-release, low risk
- Miracle-Gro All Purpose (24-8-16) — fast early-season; STOP by July 1
- Shake 'n Feed Citrus/Avocado/Mango (8-2-10) — mid-to-late season, supports fruit quality
- Jack's Classic 20-20-20 — precision feeding, medium risk
- Triple 10 Granular (10-10-10) — budget in-ground; not recommended for containers
- Potassium Sulfate / SOP (0-0-50) — midsummer K boost

### Grower Programs by Type
- **Beginner/Container:** Osmocote Plus (Apr + Jun) + worm castings top-dress
- **Intermediate/Mixed:** Espoma Citrus-Tone (Apr, Jun) + Neptune's Harvest every 2 weeks (Apr–Jun)
- **Advanced/Collector:** Jack's 20-20-20 (Apr–Jun biweekly) → Shake 'n Feed (Jun–Aug) + SOP (Jul)
- **In-ground established:** Compost mulch ring (Mar) + Espoma Citrus-Tone (Apr, Jun) + Triple 10 if growth is slow

### Pro Tips to Work Into Chapter 7
- "Feed the soil, not just the tree" — organics build living soil that outperforms synthetics long-term
- Match nutrition to growth stage, not just the calendar — watch the tree
- Osmocote + fish emulsion: the container combo (baseline + gap-filling)
- Yellowing is not always deficiency — check moisture, drainage, pH first
- Hardening off matters as much as feeding — stop N in late summer
- Match fertilizer to variety vigor: high-vigor varieties (LSU Gold) need less N;
  standard-vigor (Brown Turkey) follow standard rates
- Keep a fertilizer log — two seasons of data beats any general guide

### Salt Flush Protocol (for containers)
1. Water slowly until draining from bottom
2. Continue adding plain water equal to 3x pot volume
3. Allow to drain completely
4. Wait 5–7 days before resuming at half-strength
5. Switch to lower-concentration formula going forward

### TDS Meter Note
A TDS meter measures salt concentration in container runoff. Healthy range: 800–1,500 ppm.
Above 2,000 ppm: flush immediately. Cost: $10–15.
