## Chapter 2 — Know Your Fig
**Status: DRAFT — Not yet approved. Do not mark concepts as locked until approved.**

### Concepts Explained *(draft — pending approval)*
- Ficus carica / mulberry family classification
- Native range (western Asia, Mediterranean)
- Fig as deciduous shrub or small tree; 20–30 feet if unmanaged
- Multi-stemmed growth form in the South
- Leaf description (lobed, 3–5 fingers, rough top, slightly fuzzy underside)
- USDA hardiness zones (Zone 7 south; Zone 6 with protection)
- Dormancy requirement (Mediterranean, not tropical — dormancy drives fruit production)
- Growth rate (4–8 feet first season in ground; slower in containers)
- Establishment timeline (3 years to solid establishment, 4–5 years to real production)
- Lifespan (50–100 years; outlives the grower if protected from winter dieback)
- Root system (shallow, spreading, beyond drip line — do not cultivate close)
- Suckering triggered by root zone disturbance
- Container spacing vs. ground spacing (10–15 feet between trees)
- Syconium (inverted hollow receptacle — flowers inside, not visible)
- Ostiole (eye — role in pollination, pests, rain entry)
- Fig honey (syrupy liquid at ostiole at peak ripeness)
- Internal variation by variety (color, texture, floret density)
- Fig wasp (Blastophaga psenes) — relationship with caprifigs and Smyrna-type figs
- Caprification (intentional — California Smyrna fig industry history)
- Parthenocarpic varieties — no wasp required for backyard growers
- Enzyme breakdown of wasp in fig before ripening
- Breba crop (previous season's wood, ripens June Zone 8, larger figs, sometimes lower flavor)
- Main crop (current season's growth, mid-summer through fall)
- Breba vs. main crop by variety — not every tree delivers both reliably
- Winter protection preserving old wood = preserving breba potential
- Pruning implications of two-crop biology
- Figs do not ripen off the tree
- Neck droop as primary harvest indicator
- FigCrack (skin splitting) as ripeness indicator
- Color change as variety-specific ripeness cue
- Fig honey at ostiole = pick now
- Split figs — peak flavor, short window, pick before rain
- Latex (milky sap) — Ficus family characteristic
- Latex skin irritation (repeated contact)
- Latex issue for grafters — score rootstock before joining
- Latex behavior by wood type (active growth bleeds more; dormant barely bleeds)
- No blossom season (no visible flowers — internal)
- Two-crop setup = paying attention to different wood at different times
- Harvest routine: daily morning walk during season
- Propagation from cuttings (hardwood cutting → productive tree, no grafting required)
- Cutting-sharing culture in the fig community
- Fig attracts a certain kind of grower (patient, curious, detail-oriented)

- "A fig that never goes dormant won't perform the way it's supposed to."
- "fig just follows the wood" (breba story conclusion)

- "walk the trees" (harvest routine — established as Randall's practice)
- "One morning you walk out and there are small green figs showing up along the branches. You didn't see them form. They're just there." (no blossom season)
