## CHAPTER 4 — Choosing the Right Fig for You

**From video — practical selection advice:**
- For beginners just starting out: Chicago Hardy, Brown Turkey — "introductory level figs without having to go all in"
- For a good off-the-shelf fig: Violette de Bordeaux, Olympian, White Marseilles — all available at big box stores
- For cold climates: Olympian ("cold hardy type"), Texas BA1 ("cold hardy Smith")
- For container growing: figs sold in tree pots (4x9) are standard for shipping and container culture
- For someone wanting fruit fast: buy an established tree, not cuttings. "An established tree comes with a root system that's ready to go, and you might get fruit the same year."

## CHAPTER 5 — Planting and Growing Figs Right

**Orchard spacing confirmed:**
- 12 feet apart in offset pattern (trees offset 6 feet between rows)
- 10 feet from fence line on each side
- 15 trees per row, 12 feet apart

**Soil confirmed:**
- South Alabama: approximately 1 foot of nice loamy topsoil, easy to dig
- "If we were dealing with heavier soil I'd dig bigger holes and backfill with loose soil around the plant" — good container-to-ground tip

**Irrigation confirmed:**
- Drip ring irrigation on timers, trenched underground
- Allows grass to grow over drip rings to protect from mower
- Without irrigation, dry summers severely limit fruit production (Year 1 lesson from his orchard)

**Pruning goal confirmed:**
- Target: 3 to 4 main stalks from base in a bush format
- "Three stalks, kind of a bush format" — for production and future big-tree propagation
- Trims everything else as cuttings

**Bad winter on record:**
- Got down to 15°F, everything died to the ground in the orchard
- "All the growth you see is this year's growth" (Oct. 2025 video)
- That's tip dieback AND root-level kill from an extreme cold event — good real example for Ch. 9

## CHAPTER 8 — Propagation

**Randall's full method — confirmed from video:**

| Step | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Timing | Late fall / early winter in Zone 8b-9 (October-November); "perfect time for rooting cuttings" when temps are 68°F nights, 80-82°F days |
| Wood type | Lignified (hardened brown wood from current year) — NOT soft green growth |
| Cutting size | ~6 inches, minimum 3 nodes |
| Bottom cut | Quarter inch below a node |
| Labeling | Pencil on pot — doesn't fade in sun, doesn't smear when wet |
| Waxing | Tops only. Wax blend: gulf/canning wax + beeswax (for pliability) + toilet wax ring (to stretch supply and add stick). Heated in a deep cylindrical stainless pot in a thrift-store soup warmer (~$20). Quick dip in, quick dip out. |
| **DO NOT wax both ends** | Confirmed failure: 5 rooted out of 75 cuttings when both ends waxed. Viewer suggestion he tried and abandoned. |
| Rooting hormone | Hormodin 3 (8% IBA). Dust lightly — do NOT wet the cutting first. Too much IBA can inhibit root development. |
| Rooting medium | Promix BX (well-draining, contains mycorrhizae). Do NOT use moisture-retaining potting mix. |
| Pots | 2" nursery pots in 32-count trays (with drain holes — no standing water) |
| Environment | 50% shade cloth. Keep out intense sun but still have light. Heated/ventilated greenhouse. |
| Up-potting | Once rooted (~2 months), move to 4x9 tree pots. Ready to sell/ship at that size. |
| Success rate | 94% with this method (late summer cuttings, his best batch). Average for new growers without greenhouse: ~50%. |

**Cuttings vs. trees — Randall's recommendation:**
- New growers without a proper rooting environment: buy established trees, don't gamble on cuttings
- Cuttings: $7–$12 each, 50% success rate for beginners = expensive
- Established trees: $30–$40 (common varieties), up to $100–$150 for rare varieties
- Benefits of cuttings: cheaper per variety, can root extras to sell back and recover costs
- Shipping concern: established trees can suffer transplant shock, soil shifts in transit

**Cutting sources mentioned:**
- FigBid (like eBay for figs)
- proigusa.com (Randall has used with good results)
- Fig gathering in Chattanooga, Tennessee (IAS-type event — confirm name before printing)
- Local trades and gifting (community culture)

## CHAPTER 9 — Getting Through Winter

**Bad freeze on record (confirmed from video):**
- Winter 2024-25: Got down to 15°F
- Everything in the orchard died all the way back to the root
- "All the growth you see here is this year's growth" (October 2025 orchard tour)
- Some varieties came back later than others — Cadum Negra Ramada didn't push until almost summer
- Smith did not come back at all (twice). Very cold-sensitive.
- Some varieties confirmed cold-sensitive: Smith, Cadum Ramada (struggling), various Mediterranean types
- Texas BA1 as a cold-hardy substitute for Smith confirmed

**Protecting in-ground trees:**
- Keeping old wood matters for breba crop — if the tree dies to ground, no breba that year
- Trees in ground 3 years have established root systems that usually survive if given protection
