## What the Tone Is NOT

- Not preachy or over-inspirational
- Not dry or overly academic
- Not a Wikipedia article with a warm sentence tacked on
- Not generic "[topic] advice" content that could have been written about any book on this subject

---

## Sentence Style

- Prefer shorter, cleaner sentences over complex run-ons
- Use plain language. If a simpler word exists, use it.
- Use real specifics: names, sizes, temperatures, timelines — not vague generalities
- Occasional longer sentences are fine for storytelling passages — keep them under control

---

## Personal Stories

- Only include stories the author has confirmed or provided
- Do not invent personal details, experiences, or events
- When a personal story slot exists in an outline, note it as `[AUTHOR'S STORY: topic]` and ask

---

## Paragraph Length

- Avoid walls of text
- 3–5 sentences per paragraph is a good target for most sections
- Longer paragraphs are acceptable for narrative or storytelling passages

---

## Headers & Structure

- Use clear section headers within chapters
- Readers should be able to skim headers and get a sense of what the chapter covers
- Do not over-subdivide — keep the book feeling like a readable narrative, not a manual

## Post-Draft Voice Check
*(Run this on every section before moving to the next one)*

### Step 1 — Banned Phrase Scan
Search the draft for these and rewrite every hit:
- Hedge qualifiers: "it's important to note," "many [practitioners] find," "in general," "generally speaking"
- Politeness inflation: "wonderful," "exciting," "a great option," "transformative," "nuanced"
- Generic voice: "vibrant," "lush," "bountiful," "thriving," "robust," "flourish"
- Bureaucratic filler: "ensure," "utilize," "leverage," "facilitate," "implement"
- AI padding: "delve into," "let's explore," "on this journey," "as we've discussed"
- Adverb problems: dramatically, remarkably, entirely, incredibly, simply (as emphasis)
- [Add your topic-specific banned phrases here]

### Step 2 — AI Structure Check
Read the section at the paragraph level. Does every paragraph have an intro sentence, three
balanced points, and a wrap-up? That pattern signals AI-generated structure. Fix it:
- Break one paragraph into a single sentence standing alone
- Let one paragraph run longer than the others
- Cut the wrap-up sentence from at least one paragraph and let the last point be the last point

### Step 3 — Specificity Check
Flag any sentence that could have been written about any book on this topic by any author.
The test: if you replaced your subject with a related topic and it still reads fine, it's too generic.
Every section should contain at least one:
- Specific name (variety, person, place, product)
- Specific date, measurement, or timeline
- Something only the author would know from their own experience

### Step 4 — Rhythm Check
If every paragraph in the section is 3–5 sentences, break that pattern on purpose.
Add a one-liner. Let something run long. Read the section aloud and listen for monotony.

### Step 5 — Read-Aloud Gate
Required before marking any section complete. Read it out loud as if talking to another
practitioner in the field. Flag every sentence that:
- You wouldn't actually say out loud
- Sounds like a brochure or press release
- Uses vocabulary you'd only see in a written article, not a conversation

Rewrite those sentences until they pass. This step catches what every other check misses.
