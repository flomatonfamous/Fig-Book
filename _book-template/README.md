# Nonfiction Book Writing System — Starter Kit

> **Created by Randall White / Flomaton Famous**
> A reusable, Cursor-native workflow for writing nonfiction books in your authentic voice
> without sounding like AI wrote them.

---

## What This Is

This is a structured folder system for writing a nonfiction book using AI as a drafting
and research assistant while keeping the voice, stories, and judgment yours. It is not
software. It is a set of plain markdown documents that act as the book's "brain."

Every document here is a template — filled with placeholders and instructions.
Your job is to replace the placeholders with your book's real content.

The system was developed while writing *The Backyard Fig* by Randall White and refined
across two chapters of real manuscript work.

---

## Quick Start

**Step 1 — Copy this folder** into your new book project. Rename `_book-template/` to
something meaningful (e.g., `fig-book/`, `chicken-book/`, etc.).

**Step 2 — Fill out the foundation files first, in this order:**
1. `PROJECT.md` — the book bible (purpose, reader, locked decisions)
2. `STYLE_GUIDE.md` — writing rules and voice
3. `research/voice-profile.md` — your personal voice, captured from real samples
4. `research/terminology.md` — canonical terms for your topic
5. `CHAPTER_LOG.md` — chapter status tracker

**Step 3 — Capture your personal stories** in `notes/personal-stories.md` before drafting
anything. These are your unfair advantage. Write them rough and raw — AI can organize
around them, but it cannot generate them.

**Step 4 — For each chapter, follow the Chapter Workflow** in `PROJECT.md` step by step.
Build the dossier. Get it approved. Then draft section by section.

## Folder Structure

```
[book-name]/
├── PROJECT.md                    ← Book bible, locked rules, chapter workflow
├── STYLE_GUIDE.md                ← Voice rules, banned phrases, Post-Draft Voice Check
├── CHAPTER_LOG.md                ← Chapter approval status and session log
├── README.md                     ← This file (rename/remove for final project)
│
├── research/
│   ├── voice-profile.md          ← Your voice: cadence, phrases, what to avoid
│   ├── terminology.md            ← Canonical usage for recurring terms
│   ├── source-ledger.md          ← Claim → source → confidence → chapter
│   ├── repetition-log.md         ← Concepts and stories used, by chapter
│   ├── transcript-ingest-rules.md ← Rules for using personal content (videos, notes, etc.)
│   └── [topic]-notes.md          ← One research file per subject area
│
├── notes/
│   └── personal-stories.md       ← Your stories, written by you, raw
│
├── docs/
│   └── chapter-dossier-template.md ← Pre-draft checklist (copy per chapter)
│
└── manuscript/
    └── 01-chapter-1.md           ← Drafts live here
```

## The Core Principle

You are the author. AI is the research assistant, first-draft typist, and consistency
checker. Nothing gets drafted until the inputs are solid. Nothing moves forward without
your approval.

**What makes text sound like AI wrote it:**
- Symmetrical structure in every section (intro → three points → wrap-up)
- Hedge-and-qualify reflex ("many growers find," "it's important to note")
- Zero specificity (no real names, dates, places, measurements)
- Even, undifferentiated rhythm (no fragments, no long runners, no personality)
- Generic examples that apply to anyone's book on this topic

**What actually fixes it:**
1. Your stories written by you first — rough, in your words, before AI touches them
2. Voice profile loaded as context every single drafting session
3. Post-Draft Voice Check run after every section (see `STYLE_GUIDE.md`)
4. Read-aloud pass before marking any section complete
